In a rural village in Wayanad, Kerala, the story begins with two people born and raised in the same soil: K. Shankar and Shadevan.

They grew up side by side, yet they learned to see the world in different ways. Both call themselves communists, but Shankar believes in communism as equality, while Shadevan takes from communism its power.

As students, Shankar was the one people loved, because his affection seemed to extend to everyone, and Shadevan found himself second each time. Even in elections, the first name people spoke was Shankar’s.

Shadevan, for all his anger, courage, and ability to fight, carried a kind of strength that showed itself in confrontation. Shankar, instead, won over opposition with words—while Shadevan won with physical force and rage.
